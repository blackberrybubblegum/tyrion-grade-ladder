      How grade boundaries work

      What is a grade boundary?

      A grade boundary is the minimum raw mark needed for a grade. Scoring the boundary mark or above earns that grade; one mark below drops you to the grade beneath it.

      Why do boundaries change every year?

      Exam boards set boundaries after marking so a grade means the same standard each year. Harder papers get lower boundaries, easier ones higher.

      How do I use this with a past paper?

      Sit the paper under timed conditions, mark it with the official mark scheme, then choose the same series and paper here and enter your total. Use the whole-subject option once you've done every paper.

      Which boards and years are covered?

      AQA GCSE from November 2014 to November 2025 and Edexcel GCSE from June 2017 to November 2025, with whole-subject and individual paper boundaries. Older AQA series include legacy A*–G unit boundaries.

      What do grades like 7-6 mean?

      Combined Science is a double award, so it's graded on a 17-point scale from 9-9 down to 1-1. A 7-6 counts as two GCSEs, one at grade 7 and one at grade 6.

      What does "out of" mean when a paper appears twice?

      Some subjects, like Art & Design, publish boundaries for both raw marks and scaled marks. They're listed separately with their totals so you can pick the one that matches your paper.
